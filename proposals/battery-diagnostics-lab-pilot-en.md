# 1. Project Summary

MCBI’s ultimate goal is to establish a battery diagnostics, testing, and critical minerals recovery research center in Mongolia, and this project is its first stage — a collection, diagnostics, and sorting laboratory.

As the use of electric vehicles, energy storage systems, electric tools, and portable devices grows in Mongolia, the need to safely manage the resulting flow of end-of-life batteries is increasing. However, practical capacity to record batteries by type, chemistry, technical condition, and hazard level, to evaluate reuse potential on an evidence basis, and to route them to the appropriate next destination remains insufficient.

Over 18 months, the project will establish one pilot center and a limited collection network to test the receive–quarantine–safe storage–diagnose–classify–record workflow. The center will not forcibly dismantle, shred, or chemically extract metal from batteries. Instead, it will place each battery into a decision category: reusable, requiring transfer to specialized processing only, or hazardous/damaged and to be quarantined.

| **Core value of the project:** It builds real data, safety practices, trained personnel, and an institutional roles model in Mongolia, making it possible to base the next investment decision on evidence rather than assumption. |

# 2. Context and Problem Rationale

## 2.1 Pressing Issues

- More than 77% of Mongolia’s territory is currently affected by land degradation, and an uncontrolled flow of end-of-life batteries risks accelerating this degradation further.

- Mongolia currently has no legal framework for mandatory battery collection, take-back, or Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for manufacturers/importers, so the flow continues to accumulate unregulated.

- Mongolia has not yet developed full-scale domestic production capacity to separate, assay, and purify rare earth elements and other critical minerals.

- Consolidated data on the actual volume, type, location, and age profile of battery waste is scarce.

- Mixing damaged or charged batteries with ordinary waste, or storing them in unsuitable locations, carries the risk of fire, thermal runaway, and hazardous substance release.

- Capacity to distinguish, by technical criteria, between batteries that can be reused and those that require reprocessing is weak.

- The roles of importers, users, repair shops, transporters, and waste operators, and the mechanism for exchanging information among them, are unclear.

- Treating an undiagnosed battery outright as either “waste” or “valuable raw material” can lead to poor safety and economic decisions.

## 2.2 Project Rationale

Before a large-scale reprocessing plant can be planned outright, the battery flow, chemical composition, SOH, logistics, safety practices, and market channels need to be tested in practice. The pilot center will close this information gap and form the basis for the next decisions on policy, standards, and investment.

## 2.3 Policy and Permitting Principles

In implementing the project, applicable Mongolian requirements on the environment, waste, hazardous substances, fire safety, occupational safety, construction, and transport will be verified with the relevant authorities. Activities requiring a permit will begin only after the permit has been obtained. Final classification, licensing, and reporting obligations will be confirmed with legal and technical advisors.

# 3. Goal and Objectives

## 3.1 Overall Goal

Lay the foundation of a national system for safely, responsibly, and traceably managing end-of-life batteries, and validate the model for a pilot collection, diagnostics, and sorting center (laboratory).

## 3.2 Specific Objectives

1.  Conduct baseline research on battery sources, types, volumes, and movement.

2.  Develop the technical, safety, and site design of the pilot center, and map out the permitting pathway.

3.  Test, within a limited scope, the system for receiving, transporting, quarantining, and storing batteries.

4.  Carry out visual inspection, voltage, temperature, and insulation checks, and — where possible — BMS data readout and SOH assessment, under standard procedures.

5.  Assign each battery a unique digital ID/QR code and record its origin, analysis, classification, and transfer.

6.  Deliver a training package for operators, emergency services, repair providers, and importers.

7.  Develop recommendations on EPR, standards, financing, and next-stage capacity, based on pilot data.

# 6. Implementation Methodology and Work Packages

## WP1. Baseline Research and Regulatory Mapping (Months 1–3)

- Conduct surveys, interviews, and voluntary data collection on battery sources.

- Develop a provisional taxonomy by chemistry, form factor, and use-case category.

- Produce a matrix of relevant laws, permits, standards, and reporting requirements.

- Establish site-selection criteria and carry out a preliminary risk assessment.

## WP2. Center Design, Permitting, and Procurement (Months 2–7)

- Plan the flow between clean/receiving/quarantine/diagnostics/storage/emergency zones.

- Develop requirements for fire detection, thermal monitoring, ventilation, spill containment, and access control.

- Prepare equipment technical specifications, obtain three price quotes, and evaluate procurement options.

- Secure permits, insurance, and service contracts.

## WP3. SOPs, Training, and Emergency Preparedness (Months 4–9)

- SOPs for receiving, rejecting, labeling, packaging, transporting, quarantining, diagnosing, classifying, and recording data.

- Response procedures for thermal runaway, smoke, mechanical damage, electrolyte leakage, and electrical hazards.

- Initial and refresher operator training; practical drills; competency assessment.

## WP4. Pilot Collection and Diagnostics (Months 8–15)

- Test hand-over channels with a small number of partner collection points.

- Register each battery under a unique ID, recording photos, origin, date, chemistry, and hazard status.

- Carry out non-destructive diagnostics and assign an A/B/C/D decision classification.

- Maintain chain-of-custody until transfer to an approved next holder.

## WP5. Evaluation, Policy Recommendations, and Scale-up Mapping (Months 14–18)

- Analyze cost, safety performance, collection/return rates, and the classification breakdown.

- Assess EPR financing, importer participation, and deposit/return scheme options.

- Estimate next-stage capacity requirements from actual flow data.

- Present the final report, policy recommendations, and a phased investment plan.

# 7. Technical Operating Model

| **Step**                 | **Action**                                                                  | **Output**                         |
|--------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| 1\. Advance Notification | Obtain information on source, type, damage, and quantity                    | Preliminary accept/reject decision |
| 2\. Receiving            | Documentation, photos, weight, labeling, external inspection                | Unique ID and record               |
| 3\. Quarantine           | Isolate units that are damaged, overheating, or leaking                     | Hazard level                       |
| 4\. Diagnostics          | Voltage, temperature, and insulation checks; BMS/SOH readout where possible | Technical result                   |
| 5\. Classification       | Decide using pre-approved criteria                                          | A/B/C/D classification             |
| 6\. Storage              | Segregate by chemistry, condition, and hazard level                         | Safe temporary storage             |
| 7\. Transfer             | Confirm documentation, recipient, date, weight                              | Chain-of-custody record            |

## 7.1 Proposed Decision Classification

| **Category** | **Description**                                                 | **Next Destination**                                 |
|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| A            | Passes diagnostics and can be reused in a validated application | Further engineering validation / second-life testing |
| B            | Requires repair or detailed module-level evaluation             | Approved specialist partner                          |
| C            | Not reusable; routed to material-recovery stream                | Approved recycling operator                          |
| D            | Damaged, overheating, leaking, or of undetermined high risk     | Quarantine and special handling                      |

Classification thresholds will be set according to battery chemistry, manufacturer technical data, test-equipment precision, intended use, and professional engineering judgment. A single, universal SOH threshold will not be applied to all batteries.

# 14. Environment, Social, and EHS

## 14.1 Minimum Safety Requirements

- A battery without a completed risk assessment will not be admitted to the main diagnostics zone.

- Batteries that are damaged, overheating, swollen, or leaking will be immediately flagged and quarantined.

- High-voltage work will require authorization, lockout-tagout procedures, and a two-person rule.

- PPE will be selected based on hazard assessment, with fit and usage records maintained.

- Emergency exits, communications, first aid, and fire response will be coordinated with the relevant authorities.

- Children and unauthorized persons will not be permitted into the operational area.

## 14.2 Environmental Protection

- Flooring, storage, and spill-containment solutions will be designed to block pathways for electrolyte and other contamination.

- Separate records will be kept for batteries, contaminated PPE, spill-response materials, and packaging.

- Processing that uses water or generates wastewater is outside the scope of the pilot.

- Transport distances, packaging, and load units will be logged to assess logistics impact.

## 14.3 Social Safeguards and Grievance Resolution

Local residents, staff, and partner organizations will have a confidential channel for raising concerns related to safety, labor, the environment, and information. A procedure for logging grievances, assigning an owner, resolving them within a set timeframe, and retaining evidence of closure will be approved at project start-up.

# 19. Conclusion and Partnership Proposal

The pilot center proposed by MCBI is a foundational investment for launching Mongolia’s battery circular system in a realistic, safe, and phased way. Rather than promising a large-scale plant upfront, the project first builds an understanding of battery flows, manages risk, and develops diagnostic capacity, traceable data, trained people, and institutional cooperation.

MCBI proposes to work with government agencies, importers, technology suppliers, universities, laboratories, development organizations, investors, and professional waste operators in the following areas:

- A site, permitting, and regulatory working group;

- Technical partnership on equipment, methodology, and training;

- A pilot collection network and battery-flow agreements;

- Digital records, QR codes, and data standards;

- Financing, in-kind support, and joint research;

- Independent safety and results evaluation.

In the long run, managing battery flows properly will not only reduce environmental risk but also form the foundation for bringing the mineral wealth needed for the future energy transition back to Mongolia with added value. All of this begins with reliably establishing the pilot center and building the first data, practices, and trust — and that is precisely the core of MCBI’s proposal.
